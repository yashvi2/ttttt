    My approach

    I move between the details and the bigger picture.

    Six modes of work. I move back and forth between them as the evidence changes. It's a loop, not a checklist.

      Iterate
      as needed

      1. User interviews, stakeholder interviews, observation, surveys, secondary research and contextual inquiry.

      2. Problem definition, synthesis, thematic analysis, journey mapping, personas and opportunity areas.

      3. Ideation, co-design, information architecture, wireframes, prototypes and service concepts.

      4. Usability testing, heuristic evaluation, accessibility testing and iterative research.

      5. High-fidelity design, design systems, documentation, developer collaboration and design QA.

      6. Analytics, feedback, post-launch evaluation and iteration.

      What I bring

      Five principles I work by.

      • Research-led

        I use evidence to understand what people actually need rather than designing around assumptions.

      • Systems thinking

        I look beyond individual touchpoints to understand the services, organisations and systems surrounding them.

      • Clarity

        I turn complex information, processes and requirements into experiences people can understand and use.

      • Collaboration

        I work across design, research, technology, business and other disciplines to move ideas towards implementation.

      • Accessibility

        I consider accessibility and inclusion as part of the design process, not an afterthought.

      About

      I'm interested in what happens between people, products and systems.

      I started in product design, learning how physical things get made and used. That curiosity followed me into UX and UI.

      At TCS I worked on enterprise projects for large healthcare and pharmaceutical organisations. The interfaces mattered, but the hardest problems usually sat around them: in handovers between teams, in regulation, in how information was produced and approved.

      That pulled me towards research, service design and systems thinking, and to an MA in Design Management in London. Today my practice combines all four. I'm still learning which question to ask first.
